1. Establish and Document a Valid Lawful Basis for Data Processing

Under Section 25 of the NDPA 2023, data collection requires an explicit, pre-defined legal justification before any processing occurs. Organizations cannot collect personal data simply because it is operationally convenient.

For executive teams, this requires a cross-functional data-mapping exercise across key corporate departments:

Human Resources

Must ensure employee data collection is tied directly to contractual necessity or legal obligations.

Finance & Operations

Must map customer transactions and vendor details to clear contractual frameworks.

Marketing & BD

Must establish airtight mechanisms for freely given, specific, and informed Consent, moving away from pre-ticked boxes or ambiguous opt-ins.

Strategic Action

Audit all departmental data entry points. Update your public-facing and internal privacy notices to clearly articulate why you collect specific data points and exactly which lawful basis justifies that processing.

2. Formally Appoint and Empower a Data Protection Officer (DPO)

If your organization processes sensitive personal information or handles data at scale, you are legally classified as a Data Controller or Processor of Major Importance (DCMI). A core legal mandate for DCMIs is the formal designation of a qualified Data Protection Officer.

A common executive misstep is assigning the DPO role as an extra task to an already overburdened IT manager or legal counsel. The NDPA requires the DPO to have structural independence. Their responsibilities include:

  • Independently monitoring internal adherence to NDPA statutory mandates and NDPC regulatory directives.
  • Serving as the official, direct liaison to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC).
  • Guiding executive leadership on privacy-by-design and privacy-by-default operational frameworks.
Strategic Action

Evaluate your data processing volume. If you meet the DCMI thresholds, formally appoint a DPO. Ensure they have a direct reporting line to executive leadership (the C-suite or Board) to eliminate operational conflicts of interest.

3. Implement a Strict 72-Hour Breach Notification Protocol

The NDPA 2023 shifts incident response from a reactive, internal discussion to a strictly regulated countdown. Section 40 mandates that in the event of a personal data breach, Data Controllers must formally notify the NDPC within 72 hours of becoming aware of the security incident.

Furthermore, if the breach is likely to result in high risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals (such as compromised financial records, identity data, or biometrics), you must notify the impacted data subjects directly without undue delay.

Strategic Action

Task your Legal, Compliance, and IT Security teams with co-authoring an updated Incident Response Plan. Conduct regular tabletop exercises to ensure your team can effectively contain a breach, assess risk thresholds, and compile a compliance-ready report for regulators within that narrow 72-hour window.

4. Integrate Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) into Governance

Section 28 mandates that organizations conduct a formal Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) prior to launching any processing activity that carries a high risk to individuals' privacy.

This requirement is particularly critical for founders driving digital transformation and public sector leaders deploying citizen-facing technology. A DPIA must be executed before:

  • Deploying new automated tracking, profiling, or AI-driven decision systems (e.g., automated credit scoring or risk underwriting algorithms).
  • Implementing biometric access controls or processing large-scale healthcare data.
  • Undertaking major cloud migrations or engaging in new cross-border data transfers.
Strategic Action

Build a mandatory DPIA trigger checklist into your procurement and project management lifecycles. Ensure no new software, system, or vendor contract goes live until its privacy risks have been formally assessed and mitigated.

5. Execute Annual Compliance Audits via a Licensed DPCO

Compliance with the NDPA 2023 is a continuous operational standard, verified by mandatory reporting. Organizations meeting regulatory thresholds must audit their systems annually and file a Compliance Audit Return (CAR) with the Commission.

Crucially, the law dictates that these audits cannot be entirely self-certified. High-risk organizations must engage a licensed Data Protection Compliance Organization (DPCO)—such as professional legal or management consultancies authorized by the NDPC—to independently review, validate, and submit their compliance returns.

Strategic Action

Review your organizational filing calendar. Engage a licensed DPCO early in your operational year to conduct a thorough gap analysis, refine your privacy infrastructure, and secure your statutory filing without the risk of late penalties.
